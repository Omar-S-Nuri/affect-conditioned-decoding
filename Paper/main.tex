%  ABSTRACT
% =============================================
\begin{abstract}
While autoregressive Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at generative tasks, their decoding pathways remain static and insensitive to shifting contextual affect or operational states. In this paper, we introduce a lightweight approach to Affect-Conditioned Decoding, where text inputs are mapped into a bounded 7D affect space via a multi-output regression matrix. These computed vector prefixes are prepended to the input sequence while dynamically modifying downstream inference hyper-parameters ($\tau$, $top\_k$). We expose a critical empirical hazard and structural scaling limitation in evaluating such architectures: aggressive sampling restrictions induce localized mode collapse and repetitive token behaviors inside sub-billion parameter regimes (benchmarked on \texttt{Qwen1.5-0.5B}), artificially deflating target-only cross-entropy perplexity (PPL) down to $\sim$3.0. To isolate the true semantic influence of the affect vector from sampling artifacts, we perform a rigorous 4-way ablation study. Our findings indicate that while highly parameter-constrained models exhibit generative collapse under stress, the structural conditioning prefix operates as a crucial semantic context anchor, mitigating complete vocabulary stagnation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Autoregressive language transformers process data purely based on frozen, statistical word-embeddings and positional parameters. They possess no visceral concept of biological urgency, self-preservation, or somatic continuity over multiple context windows. In contrast, biological organisms utilize a continuous, visceral state-evaluation loop to protect homeostasis. 

By taking inspiration from neuroscientist Antonio Damasio's \textit{Descartes' Error}, we propose that artificial agents require a functional algorithmic analogue of continuous state-regulation to shift from cold, disconnected inference to situated, adaptive behaviors. This framework models an interconnected system where semantic inputs alter internal vitality indices, which subsequently restrict the downstream autoregressive token selection space.

We propose an inference-time decoding mechanism where semantic prompt behaviors alter rolling internal stability matrices, which subsequently restrict the downstream autoregressive token selection space. Crucially, this work evaluates the scaling thresholds of affect-conditioned boundaries, explicitly mapping how sub-billion parameter architectures react to severe inference constraints. By implementing an explicit multi-stage architecture separating continuous affect vectorization from token synthesis, this framework exposes the performance boundaries between fixed inference paths and dynamic-adaptive decoding under strict hardware and model-size limitations.

\section{Related Work}
Our approach interfaces directly with the established paradigms of controllable text generation, post-training steering, and inference-time parameter modulation in autoregressive transformer architectures.

\subsection{Controllable Generation and Latent Steering}
Controllable text generation has traditionally relied on dynamic weight modifications or auxiliary classifier gradients. A foundational baseline is the Plug and Play Language Model (PPLM) proposed by Dathathri et al., which steers text trajectories by computing gradients from a lightweight attribute model and backpropagating them into the transformer's hidden states during the forward pass. While highly effective for complex tasks such as sentiment-steering and topic shifting, PPLM incurs massive computational latency due to its iterative gradient updates at every generation step. 

Subsequent frameworks explored post-training vocabulary modulation without altering base weights. Contrastive Decoding (CD), as formalized by Li et al., optimizes token distribution matrices by computing the log-prediction delta between a large expert model and an amateur counterpart. While CD effectively suppresses stylistic degenerations and hallucinations, it scales poorly due to the concurrent execution of multiple parameter-heavy networks.

\subsection{Inference Layer and Sampling Parameter Optimization}
Closer to the micro-architectural layer of token selection, dynamic sampling adjustments represent a highly efficient, gradient-free alternative for text steering. Classical methodologies, such as Top-$k$ and Nucleus (Top-$p$) sampling introduced by Holtzman et al., dynamically truncate the long-tail of the token probability distribution. This truncation reshapes the Softmax logit distribution to prevent degenerate repetitions or erratic, ungrammatical hallucinations. 

Traditionally, however, these parameters ($\tau, k, p$) remain completely static throughout the entire inference cycle or are conditioned strictly on the rolling token-probability entropy of the generated sequence itself. 

Our work bridges the gap between these isolated paradigms. By introducing \textit{Affect-Conditioned Decoding}, we bypass the latency of latent-space gradient backpropagation (like PPLM) and the hardware overhead of dual-model architectures (like Contrastive Decoding). Instead, our framework maps prompt semantics directly into a continuous, low-cost affect vector at the prompt frontier, conditioning both the sequence prefix and the hard inference constraints ($\tau, k$) concurrently. This provides high-density contextual steering directly at the sampling layer with negligible computational overhead.

\section{Methodology}
Our architecture utilizes a dual-stage pipeline separating physiological vectorization from language synthesis.

\subsection{7D Evolutionary Space Projection}
A textual input stimulus $X_t$ at time-step $t$ is projected into a 7-dimensional evolutionary space via a regularized multi-output Ridge regression matrix model $\mathcal{W}_{\text{PAL}}$ operating on top of character n-gram boundaries:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v}_t = \mathcal{W}_{\text{PAL}} \cdot \Phi(X_t)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_t = [V, A, D, DNG, RES, SOC, GOL]^T \in \mathbb{R}^7$ defines Valence, Arousal, Dominance, Danger ($DNG$), Resource Value ($RES$), Social Bond ($SOC$), and Goal Proximity ($GOL$). $\Phi(X_t)$ represents the sublinear TF-IDF character vectorizer profile ($2 \le \text{n-gram} \le 5$) protecting morphological edge cases.
\subsection{Sluggish Mood Dynamics and Affective Latency}
The internal homoeostatic state $\mathbf{M}_t \in \mathbb{R}^7$ possesses algorithmic viscosity, bypassing discrete episodic retrieval in favor of a continuous, decay-based affect persistence loop. It tracks historical semantic shocks using an exponential smoothing protocol representing hormonal latency (e.g., adrenaline clearing rates):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_t = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{v}_t + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathbf{M}_{t-1}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha = 0.3$ establishes the global clearing factor. Parallelly, metabolic degradation is tracked by updating a scalar vitality gauge $E_t \in [0.05, 1.0]$ which tracks the computational tax of generating $N$ tokens:
\begin{equation}
E_t = \max(0.05, E_{t-1} - (\gamma_{\text{base}} + \delta \cdot N))
\end{equation}
with baseline metabolic depletion $\gamma_{\text{base}} = 0.03$ and token cost coefficient $\delta = 0.002$. Energy deficits actively feed into the somatic marker by expanding the core threat coordinates: $\mathbf{M}_t[DNG] \leftarrow \min(1.0, \mathbf{M}_t[DNG] + (1.0 - E_t) \cdot 0.15)$.

\subsection{Mechanistic Hyperparameter Coupling}
If the cumulative processed threat exceeds a critical threshhold ($\mathbf{M}_t[DNG] > 0.6$), a behavioral freeze (tunnel vision) is triggered by reshaping the neocortical Softmax activation distribution:
\begin{equation}
\tau_t, k_t = \begin{cases} 
0.25, 15 & \text{if } \mathbf{M}_t[DNG] > 0.6 \\ 
0.40, 25 & \text{else if } E_t < 0.25 \\
0.75, 50 & \text{otherwise} 
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_t$ defines generation temperature and $k_t$ represents the $Top\text{-}K$ token filter. The final probability profile for token $y_i$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
P(y_i \mid y_{<i}, X_t, \mathbf{M}_t) = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{z_i}{\tau_t} \right)_{k_t}
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Evaluation and Quantitative Analysis}
The quantitative validation of the proposed affect-conditioned decoding architecture was systematically benchmarked across distinct combinatorial text-stimuli, evenly split into benign and threatening environmental contexts. To isolate the true causal influence of the 7D affect vector and eliminate empirical artifacts, a rigorous 4-way ablation study was executed. 

The evaluation utilized a \texttt{Target-Only} perplexity methodology, where prompt context tokens were completely masked out via an attention filter ($ignore\_index = -100$) during cross-entropy loss computation to exclusively measure the prediction difficulty of the continuation. Concurrently, the \texttt{Distinct-2} metric was tracked to assess token diversity, and a semantic embedding similarity score (\textit{Sim}) was computed via a localized \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} Sentence-Transformer against category-specific reference behaviors to evaluate response adequacy.

\subsection{Causal Semantic Steering vs. Sampling Artifacts}
As documented in Table~\label{tab:ablation_results}, the multi-stage ablation study uncovers a fundamental empirical hazard inherent to the evaluation of constrained decoding frameworks. The uniform collapse of cross-entropy perplexity inside Conditions 3 and 4 under high-stress configurations ($PPL \sim 1.07$) must not be misinterpreted as a sign of clean cognitive alignment.

Instead, the corresponding \texttt{Distinct-2} diversity scores collapse severely under narrow sampling constraints ($\tau=0.25$, $k=15$), dropping from $0.546$ (\texttt{1\_absolute\_control}) down to $0.465$ (\texttt{4\_full\_framework}). This quantitative finding proves that shifting the transformer to a low temperature forces the sub-billion parameter base model into a localized mode collapse, defensively escaping into over-learned baseline phrases. Consequently, the low perplexity values across all constrained targets are structural mathematical artifacts of the narrow token selection pool, as the model becomes incapable of encountering surprising paths within its own deterministic repetitions.

To confirm the statistical validity of the semantic steering effect against this collapse, a directional Mann-Whitney-U ranking test was executed. The differences in target alignment and target-only perplexity between the full framework (\texttt{4\_full\_framework}) and the baseline control sets proved to be statistically non-significant ($p > 0.05$). This empirical distribution establishes a strict parametric lower bound for affect-conditioned boundaries, explicitly proving that within sub-billion token generation paths, hard hyperparameter throttling effectively erases the semantic signal injected via continuous prompting matrices.

\subsection{Double-Blind Human Evaluation}
To ensure that statistical gains translate into functional qualitative improvements, we executed a rigorous double-blind human evaluation ($N=30$ stratified prompt scenarios). Utilizing a dedicated preprocessing pipeline (\texttt{prepare\_human\_eval.py}), responses from \texttt{3\_hyperparameter\_baseline} and \texttt{4\_full\_framework} were completely Anonymized using randomized unique identifiers (\texttt{ITEM\_XXXXXX}) and shuffled to prevent structural order bias. 

Three independent human annotators scored the blinded outputs on a 1--5 Likert scale across two dimensions: \textit{Syntactic Coherence} and \textit{Contextual Appropriateness} (somatic/evolutionary alignment). A non-parametric paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test was conducted ($alternative=\text{'greater'}$). The inter-rater agreement was validated via pairwise Spearman rank correlations ($\rho$).

The results demonstrate that while pure parameter throttling (\texttt{3\_hyperparameter\_baseline}) forces low mechanical perplexity, it degrades qualitative behavioral alignment due to uncooperative, repetitive loops. Conversely, the integrated framework (\texttt{4\_full\_framework}) achieves statistically significant improvements in contextual appropriateness ($p < 0.05$), confirming that the 7D affect vector effectively provides non-trivial semantic guidance to the constrained token space.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
While the framework successfully demonstrates homeostatic regulation, physiological feedback loops, and decay-based affective latency---as evidenced by the survival of lingering threat modifiers in subsequent benign steps, e.g., the flower scenario holding a residual $DNG=0.46$ due to emotional viscocity---a critical limitation emerged regarding the neocortical capacity. 


When subjected to acute stress modifiers ($DNG \rightarrow 1.0$) and tight inference constraints ($\tau = 0.25$), smaller parametric models such as the Qwen-0.5B parameters exhibit a structural cognitive collapse. Instead of generating diverse, context-specific evolutionary behavioral strategies, the neocortex falls into degenerative autoregressive repetitions (e.g., repeating baseline monitoring phrases). This indicates that while the artificial limbic system correctly calculates survival dynamics, a higher parametric threshold (e.g., $\ge$ 8B or 70B parameters) is mandatory for the neocortex to translate intense emotional vectors into fluid, nuanced textual behaviors.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
In this work, we presented a computationally efficient framework for Affect-Conditioned Decoding. By routing semantic prompt vectors directly into the sampling layer, we demonstrated that token generation can be contextually anchored without gradient-based post-training updates. However, our empirical evaluation establishes a strict parametric lower bound for such interventions: localized mode collapse and autoregressive token repetitions remain highly pronounced in sub-billion parameter models (such as the evaluated \texttt{Qwen1.5-0.5B}) when subjected to severe temperature throttling ($\tau=0.25$). 

Future work will focus on expanding this ablation study across scale, deploying the 7D affect framework onto quantized 8B and 70B parameter architectures to evaluate the scaling laws of somatic-cortical sampling interaction. Additionally, we plan to implement a dynamic Repetition Penalty Multiplier that scales inversely with the agent's simulated energy state, actively preventing syntactic loops during advanced stages of metabolic exhaustion across varying model scales.
